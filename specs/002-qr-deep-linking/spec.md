## Clarifications

### Session 2026-02-05

- Q: Claim URL parameter model—single token (backend resolves) vs explicit template_id + session_token in URL? → A: Option B — Single token only. Claim URL contains one token; backend resolves it to template_id + session_token. Aligns with share-link pattern and keeps URLs short and single-use friendly.
- Q: Landing experience when app not installed—install only vs install + claim-related actions? → A: Option A — Install guidance and store links only. User is taken to a landing page (same pattern as share link's landing page URL, e.g. collection landing); no claim-related actions on web (no coupon details form, no claim on web).
- Q: Claim token in URL and exposure—accept as-is vs single-use/short-lived? → A: Option B — Claim tokens are single-use and short-lived: valid for one successful claim and expire after a short period (or after first use); spec states this to limit exposure from logging or sharing.
- Q: Legacy JSON QR format—MUST support in-app vs optional vs no legacy? → A: Option C — No legacy support. The in-app scanner only accepts deep-link URLs; legacy JSON format (raw template_id + session_token) is out of scope (breaking change for existing QRs).
- Q: Who generates the claim QR—merchant/system only vs users too? → A: Option A — Merchant/system only. The claim QR (and claim URL/token) is generated by the merchant or system (e.g. when the merchant displays a QR for a coupon template); users only scan and claim.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Scan QR Outside App and Open Coupon Claim (Priority: P1)

A user scans a coupon QR code with the device camera or a third-party QR app (outside the CouPro app). The system opens the CouPro app if installed, navigates to the claim flow, and pre-fills the coupon parameters so the user can complete the claim in one step.

**Why this priority**: Core value of deep linking—enables "scan anywhere" and matches share-link behaviour.

**Independent Test**: Scan a QR code that encodes the claim URL with a system camera or external QR reader; confirm the app opens and shows the claim flow with the correct coupon context.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the user has the app installed, **When** they scan a valid coupon-claim QR code with the system camera or an external QR app, **Then** the app opens and presents the claim flow with coupon parameters already set.
2. **Given** the user has the app installed but it was closed, **When** they open the encoded URL from a QR scan (e.g. via browser or system handler), **Then** the app launches and navigates to the claim flow with the same coupon parameters.
3. **Given** the QR code encodes a claim URL in the same format as the share link (e.g. web URL or app scheme), **When** the user opens that URL, **Then** behaviour is consistent with opening a share link for claiming.

---

### User Story 2 - In-App QR Scan Behaviour Unchanged (Priority: P1)

A user opens the in-app QR scanner and scans a coupon QR code. The existing claim flow and re-scan prevention behaviour remain exactly as today: one successful claim per QR payload per session, no duplicate claims from re-scanning the same code.

**Why this priority**: Explicit constraint from the stakeholder; preserves trust and prevents abuse.

**Independent Test**: In the app, open the QR scanner, scan a valid coupon QR, complete claim; scan the same QR again and confirm the system does not submit a second claim (re-scan prevention unchanged).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the user is on the in-app QR scanner, **When** they scan a QR code that encodes a claim URL (deep-link format), **Then** the claim logic runs and existing re-scan prevention rules apply (e.g. same payload not claimable again within the session, time/content-based guards). Legacy JSON format is out of scope; only deep-link URLs are accepted.
2. **Given** the user has just successfully claimed a coupon via the in-app scanner, **When** they scan the same QR again, **Then** the system does not submit another claim and the prevention behaviour is unchanged from the current implementation.
3. **Given** a QR code that encodes a claim URL (deep-link format), **When** scanned in-app, **Then** the mechanism that prevents re-scanning or duplicate claims is not altered or removed. QR payloads that are not deep-link URLs (e.g. legacy JSON) are not supported; the scanner accepts only deep-link URL format.

---

### User Story 3 - No App Installed: Landing Page with Install Guidance (Priority: P2)

A user scans a coupon QR code and opens the encoded URL but does not have the CouPro app installed. The system takes them to a landing page (following the same pattern as the share link landing page URL, e.g. collection landing) that shows install guidance and store links only—no claim-related actions on web.

**Why this priority**: Completes the "same as share link" experience and avoids dead ends.

**Independent Test**: On a device or emulator without the app, open the claim URL (e.g. from a QR code); confirm the landing page shows install guidance and store links (same pattern as share link landing).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the user does not have the app installed, **When** they open the claim URL (e.g. via QR scan opening the web link), **Then** they are taken to a landing page that guides them to install the app (e.g. App Store / Play Store links), following the same pattern as the share link landing page URL.
2. **Given** the landing page is shown, **When** the user has no app, **Then** they can reach the app store from the page; the landing does not offer claim-related actions (no coupon form, no claim on web).
3. **Given** the user later installs the app from the same link or store, **When** they open the same claim URL again (e.g. from history or re-scan), **Then** the app opens and they can complete the claim flow.

---

### User Story 4 - QR and Share Link Use Same Claim Parameters (Priority: P2)

Coupon claim via QR and via share link use the same parameter model: the claim URL carries a single token; the backend resolves that token to template_id and session_token internally. One claim endpoint and one set of validation rules apply. QR codes can encode either a web URL or an app-scheme URL that carries this claim token.

**Why this priority**: Ensures consistency, single source of truth, and easier maintenance.

**Independent Test**: Generate a share link for a claim and a QR that encodes the same claim; open both and confirm the same claim result and validation behaviour.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a coupon claim is available via share link, **When** a QR code is generated that encodes the same claim URL (web or app scheme), **Then** opening either link results in the same claim parameters and backend behaviour.
2. **Given** the backend accepts a claim token (resolving to template_id + session_token), **When** the user opens a QR-encoded URL with that token, **Then** the app sends the token and receives the same success or error responses as for share links.
3. **Given** invalid or expired parameters, **When** opened via QR or share link, **Then** the user sees appropriate error messaging in both cases.

---

### Edge Cases

- What happens when the user scans a QR that encodes a URL but the device has no browser and no app? System should still show the best possible guidance (e.g. store link or error message with support info).
- What happens when the claim URL is opened while the app is in background? App comes to foreground and shows the claim flow with the same claim token (and resolved parameters).
- What happens when the encoded URL is malformed or missing required parameters? User sees a clear error (e.g. "無效的連結" or "連結已過期") and no crash.
- What happens when the user scans a QR that contains non-URL content (e.g. legacy JSON) in the in-app scanner? The scanner accepts only deep-link URLs; other content is out of scope and may show a clear error or be ignored (no legacy JSON support).
- What happens when the same claim URL is opened multiple times from outside the app (e.g. re-opening the same link)? Backend and/or app handle idempotency or "already claimed" and show consistent messaging; in-app re-scan prevention is unchanged. Tokens are single-use and short-lived, so a reused or expired token shows a clear error (e.g. "連結已過期").
- What happens when the user has restricted "Open in app" for the domain? The landing page (same pattern as share link) still loads so the user can choose to open in app or go to store.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: System MUST support opening a coupon claim from outside the app by opening a URL (web or app scheme) that encodes a single claim token, so that scanning a QR with that URL opens the app and starts the claim flow when the app is installed. The backend resolves the token to template_id and session_token internally.
- **FR-002**: System MUST treat QR-encoded claim URLs and share links for the same coupon as equivalent: same claim token, same validation, and same claim outcome.
- **FR-003**: When the user opens a claim URL and the app is not installed, the system MUST present a landing page (same pattern as the share link’s landing page URL, e.g. collection landing) that guides the user to install the app (e.g. App Store / Play Store links) only—no claim-related actions on web.
- **FR-004**: The in-app QR scanner MUST retain the existing re-scan prevention behaviour (e.g. no second claim for the same QR payload in the same session, and existing time/content-based guards); this behaviour MUST NOT be changed by the deep linking work.
- **FR-005**: System MUST support QR codes that encode either a web (https) URL or an app-scheme URL for claiming, so that behaviour aligns with share links (e.g. Universal Link and custom scheme).
- **FR-010**: The in-app QR scanner MUST accept only deep-link URL format (claim URL with token). Legacy QR payloads (e.g. raw JSON with template_id and session_token) are out of scope; no backward compatibility for legacy format.
- **FR-006**: When a claim URL is opened (from QR or share link) with invalid or expired parameters, the system MUST show a clear, user-friendly error message and MUST NOT crash.
- **FR-009**: Claim tokens MUST be single-use and short-lived: each token is valid for at most one successful claim and MUST be invalidated after first use or after an optional TTL from creation (e.g. 24h), to limit exposure from URLs being logged or shared.
- **FR-007**: When the app is launched or brought to foreground via a claim URL, the app MUST navigate to the claim flow with the claim token from the URL (backend resolves to template_id + session_token) and allow the user to complete or cancel the claim.
- **FR-008**: System MUST handle duplicate opens of the same claim URL (e.g. user opens link twice) in a safe way (e.g. idempotent claim or "already claimed" message) without changing in-app re-scan prevention rules.

### Key Entities

- **Claim URL**: A URL (web or app scheme) that identifies a single coupon claim opportunity and carries a single claim token. The backend resolves the token to template_id and session_token. Used by both share links and QR codes.
- **Claim token**: The single parameter in the claim URL. Backend resolves it to template_id and session_token for the claim endpoint; same for QR and share link. Tokens are single-use and short-lived: valid for at most one successful claim, then invalidated; optionally expire after a TTL from creation (e.g. 24h). See data-model.md for enforcement rules.
- **Landing page (claim)**: The web page shown when a claim URL is opened and the app is not installed. Follows the same pattern as the share link’s landing page URL (e.g. collection landing): install guidance and store links only; no claim-related actions on web.
- **Re-scan prevention state**: In-session state used by the in-app scanner to block duplicate claims (e.g. same payload already claimed, or same payload within a time window). Out of scope for change; must be preserved.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: Users who scan a coupon QR code with an external scanner and have the app installed can open the app and reach the claim flow with parameters pre-filled in one action, with no manual re-entry of coupon data.
- **SC-002**: In-app QR scanning continues to prevent duplicate claims for the same QR payload within the same session; no regression in re-scan prevention behaviour.
- **SC-003**: Users without the app who open a claim URL (e.g. from a QR scan) reach the claim landing page (same pattern as share link landing) and can go to the app store without needing the app installed first.
- **SC-004**: Claim success and error rates for QR-originated claims are consistent with share-link-originated claims when using the same claim token.
- **SC-005**: Invalid or expired claim URLs opened from QR or share link show a clear error message; no unhandled errors or crashes.

## Assumptions

- The existing share link flow uses a web URL and an app-scheme URL; QR codes will encode one or both of these formats so behaviour is aligned.
- "Re-scan prevention" refers to the current in-app behaviour (e.g. blocking a second claim for the same QR payload in the same session and any existing time/content-based guards); the refactor will not remove or relax these rules.
- The claim URL uses a single token; the backend resolves that token to template_id and session_token. Claim tokens are single-use and short-lived (one successful claim, then expire or invalidate) to limit exposure from URLs being logged or shared. The backend exposes (or will expose) a claim flow that accepts this token whether the request originates from a share link or from an in-app QR scan; the deep linking work may require a unified URL format and routing.
- The claim "app not installed" experience is a landing page that follows the same pattern as the share link’s landing page URL (e.g. collection landing): install guidance and store links only; no claim-related actions on web.
- The in-app QR scanner accepts only deep-link URL format (claim URL with token). Legacy QR payloads (e.g. raw JSON with template_id and session_token) are out of scope; no backward compatibility for legacy format (breaking change for existing QRs that encode JSON).
- The claim QR (and claim URL/token) is generated by the merchant or system (e.g. when the merchant displays a QR for a coupon template); users only scan and claim. User-generated claim QRs or claim-link sharing are out of scope.
